Design

The diagrams below describe the implemented design. Class diagrams are partial views: they omit unrelated members and utility classes. Solid arrows show navigable associations, dashed arrows show dependencies, and hollow triangles point to a superclass or implemented interface. Labels explain the purpose of a dependency where needed.

Persistent identities

Customers, dogs and activities use persistent IDs as their identities. These IDs are assigned by the WatchDog and are stored with the records, so they remain stable across application restarts and are not affected by filtering, list positions or deletion of other records. This allows commands and relationships to refer to a record reliably even when the visible list changes.

Customer–dog ownership

LinkCommandParser accepts c/CUSTOMER_ID d/DOG_ID [DOG_ID]...: one customer field followed by one dog field containing distinct, whitespace-separated IDs. Repeated d/ prefixes are rejected. LinkCommand delegates the batch to Model.linkDogs; ModelManager forwards to WatchDog. The WatchDog looks up persistent IDs in the complete lists and validates every endpoint and ownership constraint before publishing one replacement dog list. A rejected batch throws LinkException without changing records or filters; the command translates it to CommandException for user feedback. After successful link or unlink publication, an internal assertion checks that every requested dog ID still exists and has the intended owner, or no owner for unlink. These checks do not mutate records or replace the ordinary validation of command input.

Each immutable Dog stores an optional owner customer ID. This is the single source of truth; customers do not store a duplicate dog-ID list. A dog may be unlinked or have one owner, while a customer may own any number of dogs. Linking an already-owned dog is rejected, including links to the same owner. Dog equality includes ownership, while identity remains the persistent dog ID.

UnlinkCommandParser accepts either c/CUSTOMER_ID or d/DOG_ID [DOG_ID]... with distinct, whitespace-separated dog IDs in one field; repeated dog prefixes and mixed prefixes are rejected. UnlinkCommand delegates to Model.unlinkCustomerDogs or Model.unlinkDogs and translates UnlinkException into command feedback. WatchDog validates every selected dog exists and has an owner before publishing one replacement list of unowned copies. Dogs may belong to different customers. The customer form validates the customer, selects all their dogs from the complete list, and uses the same atomic batch operation. Missing customers and customers with no linked dogs are rejected. Unlinking preserves records and filters, including a customer's last dog, and uses the existing nullable ownerId storage field without a schema change.

WatchDog.removeCustomer clears ownership of that customer's dogs, regardless of filters, preserving their persistent IDs and details. Unowned dog cards show a person icon followed by None. Removing a dog leaves its customer intact. Customer edits must preserve persistent IDs to keep links valid. Observable list updates refresh dog cards, which display customer IDs without changing search filters. Customer cards display owned dog IDs below backup contact. CustomerListPanel observes the complete dog list as well as visible customers, so these IDs remain accurate when dog cards are filtered out.

JsonAdaptedDog persists ownerId. A missing or null field means unlinked, preserving compatibility with older files. Negative owner IDs and references to missing customers are rejected on load; owner IDs must be integer JSON numbers, so fractions and numeric strings cannot silently change ownership. Customers are restored before dogs. Copies of WatchDog retain ownership through immutable dogs. The shared model boundaries also reject dangling owner IDs: setDog validates the replacement, setCustomers checks that existing owners remain present, and resetData validates ownership, customer-ID uniqueness, and identity counters against snapshots of the incoming data before changing records. Customer deletion uses its explicit unlinking path instead of removing a referenced customer through setCustomers. EditDogCommand preserves the current owner ID when constructing a replacement dog, so editing details does not unlink the dog. Ownership changes remain explicit through linking, unlinking, or customer deletion. Deleting a dog also removes its enrolment records in the same address-book operation, including past occurrences; other dogs' enrolments and activity schedules are preserved.

Dog–activity enrolment

Dog participation in an activity occurrence is represented by a separate Enrolment record containing an activity ID, a dog ID, and an occurrence date. This separates the reusable activity schedule from the dogs attending it while distinguishing occurrences of recurring activities. It also allows dogs to be enrolled or unenrolled independently without modifying the activity's schedule or storing duplicate dog objects inside Activity.

Architecture

The architecture diagram shows dependencies between the four main components. MainApp wires them together at startup. Commons supplies shared utilities; its dependency arrows are omitted.

The following provides a quick overview of the main components and their interactions.

Main components of the architecture

Main (consisting of classes Main and MainApp) is in charge of the app launch and shut down.

  • At app launch, it initializes the other components in the correct sequence, and connects them up with each other.
  • At shut down, it shuts down the other components and invokes cleanup methods where necessary.

The bulk of the app's work is done by the following four components:

  • UI: The UI of the App.
  • Logic: The command executor.
  • Model: Holds the data of the App in memory.
  • Storage: Reads data from, and writes data to, the hard disk.

Commons represents a collection of classes used by multiple other components.

UI component

The API of this component is specified in Ui.java

The UI consists of a MainWindow and its parts, including CommandBox, ResultDisplay, separate customer, dog and activity list panels, and TodayActivityListPanel. TodayActivityListPanel reuses the activity-list implementation and filters it to schedules occurring today. Visible GUI parts inherit from the abstract UiPart class, which provides their shared FXML-loading behavior.

Each list panel observes its model data and uses CardListUpdater to reconcile stable, ID-keyed card factories. Unchanged entries are retained instead of rebuilding the entire list, which preserves the visible record during ordinary edits, ownership changes, and enrolment changes. Commands that intentionally replace the displayed result set, such as search, filter, and reset, scroll the panels to the first result. JavaFX still creates card nodes only as cells need them. Customer ownership summaries use the complete dog list independently of the current dog filter.

Customer and dog cards install their tooltips and copy menus through CardInteractionUtil. Clipboard writes return a success result through ClipboardWriter, so the UI shows Copied! only after a successful write and shows failure feedback otherwise.

The UI component uses the JavaFX UI framework. The layouts of these UI parts are defined in matching .fxml files in src/main/resources/view. For example, MainWindow.fxml specifies the layout of MainWindow.

The UI component,

  • executes user commands using the Logic component.
  • listens for changes to Model data so that the UI can be updated with the modified data.
  • keeps a reference to the Logic component, because the UI relies on the Logic to execute commands.
  • depends on some classes in the Model component because it displays Customer and Dog objects from the model.

The general activity panel shows distinct dog IDs booked today or later, alongside search-specific per-dog enrolment dates when a search context supplies them. The Today panel uses all schedules and shows only schedules and bookings on the current date. ActivityBookingSummary formats these booking lines from live enrolments. Activity, enrolment, and search-context changes trigger refreshes; after a successful command, MainWindow captures one date and refreshes both activity panels with it. There is no midnight scheduler; the date is re-evaluated on the next refresh.

How the Logic component works:

  1. When Logic is called upon to execute a command, the command is passed to a WatchDogParser object, which in turn creates a parser that matches the command (e.g., DeleteDogCommandParser) and uses it to parse the command.
  2. This results in a Command object (more precisely, an object of one of its subclasses e.g., DeleteDogCommand). For an ordinary undoable command, LogicManager captures the model state before executing it. Undo, redo, and clear confirmation follow the special paths described below.
  3. The command communicates with the Model when it is executed (e.g. to find and delete a dog by persistent ID). These interactions are omitted from this Logic view and shown at component level in the architecture sequence.
  4. Successful execution produces a CommandResult. LogicManager updates history and saves the model before returning the result; help, summary, and exit preserve history.

Model component

API : Model.java

This implementation view shows stored collections separately from filtered views. Ownership and enrolment references use ID attributes; there are no direct Dog-to-Customer or Enrolment-to-Activity object references. The activity detail view below expands the schedule fields.

The Model component,

  • stores customers and dogs in UniqueCustomerList and UniqueDogList, with persistent IDs and dog ownership links.
  • stores activity schedules and dated enrolments separately; filtered activities are a view of the stored schedules.
  • exposes separate unmodifiable observable customer and dog lists for search and category filtering. The UI observes these lists to update cards when records or filters change.
  • stores a UserPrefs object that represents the user’s preferences (currently, just the GUI settings). This is exposed to the outside as a ReadOnlyUserPrefs object.
  • does not depend on any of the other three components (as the Model represents data entities of the domain, they should make sense on their own without depending on other components)

Storage component

API : Storage.java

The Storage component,

  • can save both WatchDog data and user preference data in JSON format, and read them back into corresponding objects.
  • is implemented by StorageManager, which delegates the actual JSON file access to JsonWatchDogStorage and JsonUserPrefsStorage (one class per data file).
  • depends on some classes in the Model component (because the Storage component's job is to save/retrieve objects that belong to the Model)

Removal of the legacy person feature

WatchDog uses customer and dog records. The original AB3 person records, tags, person-only commands (add, edit, delete INDEX and find), person storage adapters and unused list panels have been removed. Shared components such as WatchDog, preferences, parsing utilities, help, clear and JSON storage remain in use.

Existing JSON files may contain a legacy persons field. It is ignored on loading and omitted on the next save; its records are not converted to customers. Customer/dog records, ownership links and both next-ID counters are preserved. A file containing only legacy persons loads as an empty WatchDog book. No startup sample person records are created.

Common classes

Classes used by multiple components are in the seedu.address.commons package.

Adding dogs

AddDogCommandParser validates the dog details supplied by the user and creates an AddDogCommand. The command delegates to Model, which forwards the request through ModelManager to WatchDog.

WatchDog assigns the dog a persistent ID when it is added. The ID is allocated from a monotonically increasing counter rather than from the dog's position in the list, so deleting a dog does not change the identities of the remaining dogs and deleted IDs are never reused. The counter and dog IDs are persisted so that new dogs continue to receive unique IDs after the application restarts.

Dog IDs remain positive int values, while the next-ID counter is a long. A counter value of Integer.MAX_VALUE + 1 represents exhaustion; adddog then fails without changing the stored dogs or counter. A missing counter in legacy data is derived as one more than the largest stored dog ID, or one for an empty dog list. An explicit stored counter must be greater than every stored dog ID and cannot exceed the exhaustion value. Zero, negative, fractional, string-valued, out-of-range, or otherwise inconsistent counters are rejected rather than silently repaired. A counter may validly be more than one above the largest current dog ID because higher-ID dogs might previously have been deleted.

Adding customers

addcustomer follows the same parser–command–model flow as adddog, but requires a name, primary phone number, email address, and backup contact. AddCustomerCommandParser rejects missing, repeated, or invalid fields and creates a customer without an ID. Before adding it, AddCustomerCommand checks the complete customer list for an existing primary phone number or email address. Phone and backup contact accept eight local digits or + followed by 7 to 15 international digits (the first digit must be 1–9). Numbers beginning with +65 must have exactly eight digits after the country code. Phone defines their shared validation and normalization: spaces and hyphens between digits are removed, and +65 followed by eight digits is stored in local form. Other international numbers retain +; cards display the stored values. SearchCustomerCommandParser uses the same phone validation and normalization before the search predicate compares values exactly. Phone numbers are normalized before comparison, email comparison ignores letter case, and backup contacts may be shared. WatchDog then assigns the customer a persistent ID from its next-customer-ID counter, which is saved with the records so deleted IDs are not reused.

When restoring a ReadOnlyWatchDog, resetData derives a missing customer counter from the largest incoming customer ID. An explicit counter must exceed every incoming customer ID and cannot exceed Integer.MAX_VALUE + 1. Duplicate customer IDs and invalid counters are rejected before any stored list is changed, so these inputs cannot leave a partial reset.

Editing customers

Like editdog, editcustomer identifies the record by its persistent ID rather than its position in a filtered list. EditCustomerCommandParser requires a positive ID and at least one replacement field. The command copies the edit descriptor, combines supplied values with the stored customer's unchanged fields, and creates a replacement with the same ID. It checks the resulting primary phone and email against other customers, excluding the customer being edited so they may keep their own contact details. Model.setCustomer replaces the stored record while retaining the ID used by dog ownership links. Unlike a dog edit, there is no owner field to copy: each dog's owner ID is stored on the dog. Both replacement contact fields use the same validation and normalization as adding customers. In particular, a +65 number requires exactly eight following digits. Invalid contact details are rejected before the stored record changes, including when editing a hidden customer.

The former select dog and select customer implementations are retained under unused/@its-shoutime/ for authorship reference only. They are not routed by the active WatchDogParser and are not supported by the final product.

Undo and redo

WatchDogParser accepts undo and redo only without arguments. LogicManager handles these commands before ordinary command execution; UndoCommand.execute and RedoCommand.execute alone cannot restore history. LogicManager keeps one undo snapshot and one redo snapshot, each paired with the original command text for feedback. A successful undoable command replaces the undo snapshot with the state captured before its execution and clears the redo snapshot. View-changing commands are undoable. help, summary, and exit preserve both slots, as do parse errors and commands rejected during execution. WatchDogParser rejects arguments after clear before LogicManager enters the pending confirmation state. A valid clear then puts LogicManager into that state and returns a warning without saving or changing history. The next input must be Y or N (case-insensitive, with surrounding whitespace accepted). Invalid answers leave confirmation pending; N cancels without changing data or history. Y executes ClearCommand, discards both snapshots, and cannot itself be undone. Confirmation is consumed even if saving the cleared data fails.

Model.createState() copies the WatchDog, including its records and all three next-ID counters, and captures the active customer, dog and activity predicates and the dog predicate used for activity enrolment summaries. Model.restoreState() resets the existing WatchDog and reapplies the saved display state. Reusing the existing model and observable lists lets UI listeners continue to see updates after a restoration. It refreshes predicates twice: the first pass installs source filters, and the second re-evaluates dependent filters. This handles both dog filters whose customer predicate reads filtered dogs and customer filters whose dog predicate reads filtered customers.

On undo, LogicManager captures the current state for redo, restores the undo snapshot, moves the history slots, then saves WatchDog. redo symmetrically captures the current state for undo, restores the redo snapshot, moves the slots, and saves. An ordinary command updates history immediately after successful execution and before saving. This ordering matches the existing policy of retaining in-memory changes when a save fails: the corresponding inverse remains available, and clear still invalidates history. The disk may contain an older state, or a partially written state if an actual write had started. Missing history produces a CommandException; save failures are reported as command errors. History is held only in memory and is unavailable after a restart.

LogicManagerTest and LogicManagerHistoryTest cover the undo/redo cycle, neutral commands, invalidated history, clear, failed commands, dependent filters, and save failures. ModelManagerTest checks restoration of data, ID counters, relationships, filters, and selection, while WatchDogParserTest checks argument validation.

Daycare summary

WatchDogParser accepts summary with blank arguments only and constructs SummaryCommand. At execution, the command captures LocalDate.now(clock) once and uses that date for every count and the feedback date. The default clock uses the system's local zone; tests inject a clock. Counts use the full model lists, so filters and dog/customer selection remain unchanged.

Each stored activity schedule contributes once to the total. A schedule contributes to the from-today-onwards count when nextOccurrenceOnOrAfter(today) is present, and to today's activity count when occursOn(today) is true. Empty activities and sessions that ended earlier today are included; recurring series are never expanded into future dates.

Model.getDogsEnrolledOn(LocalDate) returns an unmodifiable snapshot. ModelManager collects dog IDs from enrolments on that date into a set, then resolves them against the full dog list in its existing order. This counts a dog enrolled in multiple activities once while keeping same-name dogs with different IDs distinct. Null dates are rejected with NullPointerException. The query takes time proportional to the number of enrolments plus dogs and stores no cached count.

Feedback follows the existing CommandResult and ResultDisplay path. No records, filters or selection are mutated, and no storage schema changes are needed. LogicManager still attempts to save after successful execution, preserving its existing save-error handling. Summary neither creates an undo entry nor discards redo history.

Activity creation and storage

The following partial class diagram expands the activity fields and separately stored enrolments. An activity represents a reusable schedule; its occurrence dates are calculated from its recurrence rule. Some parameter and return details are omitted. nextOccurrenceOnOrAfter(date) returns an Optional<LocalDate>, containing the next occurrence when one exists.

WatchDogParser routes addactivity to AddActivityCommandParser. The parser requires n/, date/, start/, end/, and r/ exactly once, rejects unknown alphabetic prefixes, and validates strict calendar dates, minute-resolution 24-hour times and lowercase recurrence. The immutable command validates a details snapshot with a placeholder ID. Parsing never allocates an ID. execute calls Model.addActivity(ActivityName, LocalDate, LocalTime, LocalTime, Recurrence) and reports the identified schedule returned by the model. No dog/customer filters change.

ModelManager delegates to WatchDog.addActivity. The WatchDog validates the complete schedule before assigning the next ID and publishing it through getActivityList(), an unmodifiable observable view of all schedules. Creation allows identical details and overlapping empty schedules. The first allocated ID is one; the monotonically increasing counter is stored as a long so that Integer.MAX_VALUE + 1 can represent exhaustion without wrapping. ID exhaustion rejects creation. Copy/reset and equality include activities and the counter. Existing customer/dog APIs are unchanged.

JsonAdaptedActivity stores the six schedule fields; dates/times use ISO strings and recurrence uses its enum name. JsonSerializableWatchDog stores activities and nextActivityId, validates identities and the counter, and rejects invalid schedules or null entries. Older files with no activity fields load an empty collection. A missing counter is derived from the largest stored ID; an explicit counter must exceed every stored ID and cannot exceed the exhaustion sentinel. Activity IDs and the counter must be integer JSON numbers; fractions, numeric strings and out-of-range numbers are rejected before conversion. Reset restores identity counters before replacing activities and enrolments. An internal activity list batches activity notifications until enrolments are restored, so listeners of either list see the restored schedules, enrolments and counters. Before publishing a reset, an isolated candidate validates incoming customer and dog identities and reuses storage enrolment validation for references, occurrence dates, duplicate bookings and overlaps. Rejected snapshots leave the existing records, counters and observable lists unchanged. Historical bookings remain valid during restoration; the restriction to today or later applies to new enrolments. LogicManager uses its existing save path after successful command execution.

Deleting an activity

WatchDogParser routes deleteactivity to DeleteActivityCommandParser, which accepts one positive persistent ID. The command finds the matching activity in Model.getActivityList() and passes it to Model.deleteActivity. ModelManager delegates to WatchDog.removeActivity, which checks that the activity is still stored, removes its enrolments, and then removes the activity. The next activity ID does not change, so deleted IDs are not reused. After the command returns its success message, LogicManager saves the updated WatchDog.

Editing an activity

WatchDogParser routes editactivity to EditActivityCommandParser. The parser requires a persistent activity ID and at least one replacement field. EditActivityCommand finds the stored activity by ID, builds an immutable replacement with the same ID, and calls Model.setActivity. Fields omitted from the command retain their stored values.

ModelManager delegates the replacement to WatchDog, which checks that the target matches a stored activity and retains its ID. Any enrolment referencing that activity rejects the edit with feedback suggesting a new activity instead. A permitted replacement then passes schedule validation before being stored. Enrolments are never removed by editing. LogicManager saves the address book after successful execution.

Dog search

WatchDogParser routes searchd to SearchDogCommandParser. The parser accepts the lowercase fields n/, b/, s/, and i/, rejects duplicate or unknown fields, normalises whitespace, and validates sizes and positive dog IDs. DogSearchPredicate performs case-insensitive substring matching for names and breeds, case-insensitive exact matching for sizes, and exact matching for IDs.

SearchDogCommand rejects IDs that are not present in the database before changing filtered lists. It applies the dog predicate, derives owners from the current matching dogs, and filters activities to schedules with at least one enrolled dog matching the same predicate. Thus unowned matching dogs remain visible while the customer list is empty, and activities with no matching enrolments are excluded. The predicates use current model data, so dog, customer, activity, and enrolment changes refresh the corresponding panels while the filter remains active. Each Enrolment stores its occurrence date. searcha with a date therefore shows only dogs enrolled on that specific occurrence, and shows their owners only when those dogs are visible. searchd shows a schedule when a matching dog is enrolled on a valid occurrence of that schedule. For searchd, the activity panel additionally receives a derived enrolment summary. It groups matching stored enrolments by dog ID, removes duplicate dates, sorts both dogs and dates deterministically, and displays the dates in ISO format. The summary includes past, present, and future dates; recurrence is never expanded into unstored dates. Other activity-card contexts retain the normal card format. Successful executions use the Successfully searched dogs by prefix and report only supplied fields. Invalid IDs and sizes use the Error in searching dogs: prefix with their specific validation messages.

Customer search

WatchDogParser routes searchc to SearchCustomerCommandParser. The parser accepts the optional lowercase fields n/, p/, e/, d/, bc/, and i/. Names are normalised for case-insensitive substring matching, while contact numbers and email addresses use exact matching. Multiple dog IDs are parsed from the d/ field and are retained as a set. The optional customer ID is parsed as a positive integer and matched exactly against existing customer IDs.

Both p/ and bc/ require complete numbers accepted by Phone.isValidPhone; partial phone searches are rejected. The parser removes spaces and hyphens and converts +65 followed by eight digits to local form before creating the command. Other international numbers retain their leading +. Consequently, searchc p/+65 9123-4567 matches a stored 91234567. An invalid contact query, including a +65 number with seven or nine following digits, fails before the command executes and leaves all current filters unchanged. Contact errors identify p/ or bc/ and distinguish empty values, invalid characters, incorrect formatting, the +65 length requirement, international numbers starting with zero, and local or international length limits. Repeated contact fields have a separate single-value error.

SearchCustomerCommand validates that the requested customer ID and every requested dog ID exist, then applies the customer ID criterion together with CustomerSearchPredicate to the filtered customer list. The predicate derives ownership from each dog's stored ownerId; a customer matches a multi-dog filter only when they own every requested dog. After customer filtering, the command derives the matching customer IDs and applies a second predicate to the dog list, showing only dogs owned by those customers. Dog IDs in the success message are sorted numerically, and customer ID is reported only when supplied. It also filters the activity panel to activities with enrolments belonging to the matching customers and supplies the same per-dog enrolment summary used by searchd. An activity with no matching enrolment is omitted, so the activity panel cannot show unrelated cards or cards with misleading empty enrolment details. Invalid or nonexistent dog and customer IDs produce specific errors under the Error in searching customers: prefix. The parser stops field values at any field-like prefix, including unsupported prefixes, so inputs such as searchc n/Anna x/value are rejected rather than treating x/value as part of the name. ModelManager reapplies active customer and dog predicates after customer edits, dog edits, and relationship changes so both filtered views remain consistent.

Activity search

WatchDogParser routes searcha to SearchActivityCommandParser. The parser accepts at least one of the lowercase fields n/, d/, t/, r/, and i/, rejects duplicate or unknown fields, and validates dates and times using strict YYYY-MM-DD and HH:mm formats. Recurrence is restricted to Once, Weekly, or Daily. Names are trimmed, internal whitespace is normalised, and matching is case-insensitive.

SearchActivityCommand (invoked by searcha) applies an ActivitySearchPredicate to the model's filtered activity list. Name matching uses a case-insensitive substring, date matching delegates to Activity.occursOn(date) so recurring activities are handled correctly, and time matching includes both endpoints. Recurrence matching compares the activity's stored recurrence. The optional activity ID must be positive and identify an existing activity, including one hidden by filters. Invalid or nonexistent IDs produce Error in searching activities: followed by Please enter a valid activity index without changing the display. ID matching is exact. Multiple fields are combined with AND semantics. The activity panel observes this filtered list, while the underlying stored schedules remain unchanged. The success message reports only the supplied fields, in the order name, date, time, recurrence, and activity ID. The activity predicate is reapplied after activity additions and activity state or enrolment changes, so the activity display remains filtered without requiring the user to repeat the command. Successful executions return Successfully searched activities by with only the supplied fields; invalid date, time, or recurrence values use the Error in searching activities: prefix. Invalid command shapes retain the existing invalid-format response with the command usage. The command also derives the dog panel from enrolments in matching activities and the customer panel from those dogs' owners. These predicates inspect current model data, so relationship changes refresh all three displays. When the filter contains a date, dog visibility additionally requires an enrolment on that exact occurrence date; the owners panel is derived from those dogs. When no date is supplied, enrolments from any occurrence may match. Gradle test execution enables Java assertions so internal model invariants are checked during verification.

Today command

WatchDogParser routes the exact lowercase command today to TodayCommandParser, which rejects any additional arguments. TodayCommand delegates the filtering behavior to SearchActivityCommand with LocalDate.now() as the date criterion. This reuses the existing recurrence-aware activity matching and derives dogs from enrolments on today's exact occurrence, followed by their owners. The command replaces the active activity, dog, and customer displays and returns Successfully displayed today's activities, as well as the dogs and customers involved for the day.

Unlinked filtering

WatchDogParser routes filterun to FilterUnlinkedCommandParser, which accepts only the lowercase categories dog and customer, with arbitrary surrounding whitespace. FilterUnlinkedCommand filters dogs whose ownerId is absent or customers with no owned dogs. The dog form hides customers and shows activities with bookings for unowned dogs; its enrolment details include only those dogs. The customer form hides both dogs and activities. Its predicates are stored by ModelManager, so the results refresh after later record or relationship changes.

Activity enrolment

Dogs are linked to activity occurrences through separate Enrolment records. Each record stores an activity ID, a dog ID, and an occurrence date, allowing an activity to have multiple dogs without storing dog objects directly inside the reusable activity schedule.

Users can enrol or unenrol one or more dogs using:

enrol a/ACTIVITY_ID date/YYYY-MM-DD d/DOG_ID [DOG_ID...]
unenrol a/ACTIVITY_ID date/YYYY-MM-DD d/DOG_ID [DOG_ID...]

The parsers accept the three required fields in any order and convert the activity ID, occurrence date, and whitespace-separated dog IDs into a command. Each field must appear exactly once, and activity and dog IDs must be positive. Parser errors identify the missing, invalid, repeated, or malformed field and append the command's complete format and example. Execution errors append the same guidance. The command delegates through Model and ModelManager to WatchDog, which verifies that the activity and dogs exist, the date is a valid occurrence and is today or later, and the requested enrolments are valid before updating the enrolment list. A dog cannot be enrolled in another activity with an overlapping time on the same date; adjacent activities are allowed. Batch operations are atomic. Successful mutations are logged at FINE and guarded by postcondition assertions. Enrolment changes are persisted with WatchDog.

The activity-card overview follows a view policy rather than changing the schedule model. Logic exposes getEnrolmentList() as the same live unmodifiable observable view supplied by WatchDog; no dog IDs are stored redundantly in Activity. MainWindow passes that view to both activity panels. The general panel formats distinct, numerically sorted dog IDs from enrolments whose date is today or later, while the Today panel uses only enrolments whose date equals the captured local date. Existing address-book invariants ensure that these enrolments refer to stored activities and dogs.

ActivityBookingSummary performs the date query and formatting without JavaFX. ActivityListPanel captures LocalDate.now(clock) once per refresh, reconciles cards when either the displayed activities or enrolments change, and accepts an injectable clock for deterministic tests. The Today variant additionally displays only schedules that occur on that captured date. Because reset, clear, undo and redo update the existing observable lists, listeners remain attached. MainWindow also requests a refresh after each successful command, so date rollover is reflected on the next successful command; no midnight scheduler is installed.

ActivityCard(Activity, String) receives the complete overview label. It presents Schedule starts separately from the booking summary and never substitutes an enrolment date for Activity.startDate. Xander's search-context work can extend the supplied presentation text or add per-dog date lines without changing the baseline live-list plumbing or storing view state in Activity.

Unenrolment follows the same parser and model delegation path. After validating the activity, date, dogs, and exact dated enrolments, WatchDog removes only the matching activity/dog/date records instead of changing the activity schedule itself. Past dates may be unenrolled for correction. Successful removal is logged and checked with a postcondition assertion.

ActivityEnrolmentTest covers recurrence validation, atomic batches, overlap boundaries and error details; the enrol and unenrol command/parser tests cover command construction and malformed or impossible dates. EnrolmentStorageTest covers persisted and legacy records. DeleteDogCommandTest checks that deleting a dog removes both past and future enrolments without affecting other dogs. ListPanelsTest and MainWindowTest cover the today-only activity filter and its UI integration. ActivityBookingSummaryTest covers empty summaries, date boundaries, activity isolation, deduplication and numeric sorting with fixed dates.

The sequence diagrams focus on interactions between application layers. Low-level list operations and exception propagation are omitted where they do not affect the overall design.

Design considerations

Use Model.getActivityList() for observing activities and Activity.getId() for identity, independently of displayed positions. Use Activity.occursOn(date) for dated schedule queries. An activity's start date is its first occurrence. Enrolments are stored separately from activity schedules and are keyed by activity ID, dog ID, and occurrence date. Legacy enrolment records without a date are migrated to their activity's first date when loaded. MainWindow displays all activities alongside a today-only panel and a date-specific heading. The panel reacts to activity-list changes through the shared observable list. addActivityFromStorage and setNextActivityId are restoration APIs, not command entry points.

Defensive programming, logging, and assertions

Command arguments and model invariants are validated explicitly and produce domain or parser exceptions; Java assertions are reserved for internal postconditions, never for user-input validation. Ownership updates assert that all requested dog endpoints exist with the expected owner after publication. Enrolment, unenrolment, and dog deletion assert that the expected dated records are present or absent after a successful mutation. The Gradle run and test tasks enable assertions so these checks are active during development and automated testing.

Customer search commands and predicates copy their query dog IDs, preventing a caller from changing an existing query through its original set. The predicate retains the live stored-dog view so later ownership changes still refresh the results. Restoration asserts that validated schedules and bookings are preserved. Recurrence and summary tests check date boundaries and schedule counts. Their FINE diagnostics contain counts and dates rather than customer contacts.

LogicManager records each command. WatchDog logs successful enrolment changes and dog deletion at FINE, including the number of enrolment records removed. An invalid enrolment restored from storage is logged at WARNING with its IDs, date, and validation reason before the load error is propagated. Expected user validation failures are returned through command feedback rather than logged as warnings, avoiding misleading error logs for routine input mistakes.

AddActivityCommandParserTest, AddActivityCommandTest, ActivityIntegrationTest and ActivityStorageTest cover parser failures, unchanged state, observable access, identity boundaries, all recurrence modes, old files and command-to-disk round trips using temporary files.

Search commands

The active category searches are searcha, searchc, and searchd. Each parser accepts its documented field prefixes and constructs the corresponding field-based command. The commands replace the relevant filtered observable list and keep related dogs, customers, and activities consistent.

SearchDogCommandParser accepts n/, b/, s/, and i/; SearchCustomerCommandParser accepts n/, p/, e/, d/, bc/, and i/; and SearchActivityCommandParser accepts n/, d/, t/, r/, and i/. Unsupported prefixes, duplicate fields, invalid IDs, invalid sizes, and malformed values are rejected before execution.

The global search command and its category compatibility routes have been retired. Use the field-based searchc, searchd, and searcha commands (i/ for persistent IDs). Retired global search code, tests, and orphaned helpers are preserved under unused/@Kenneth-Chia/. The list commands are also retired; use reset to restore all customers and dogs and upcoming activities.

Search command tests cover parser validation, field combinations, related-record updates, persistent IDs, and unchanged stored records.

Non-Functional Requirements

NFR-01. Platform compatibility: Should work on any mainstream OS as long as it has Java 25 or above installed.

NFR-02. Offline operation: After installation and setup, all core dog and customer management operations shall work without an internet connection.

NFR-03. Performance: With up to 500 dog records and 500 customer records, WatchDog shall display the result of an add, edit, delete, list or search command within 2 seconds of submission on the agreed test laptop.

NFR-04. Startup time: With the same dataset, WatchDog shall load its saved records and become ready to accept commands within 10 seconds of launch on the agreed test laptop.

NFR-05. Keyboard usability: After launch, users shall be able to perform all core dog and customer management operations using the keyboard without requiring mouse interaction.

NFR-06. Typing efficiency: A user with above average typing speed for regular English text (i.e. not code, not system admin commands) should be able to accomplish most of the tasks faster using commands than using the mouse.

NFR-07. Error clarity: For invalid command syntax, missing required fields or invalid field values, WatchDog shall display an English error message identifying the problem and providing the expected format or accepted values.

NFR-08. Robustness: An invalid command or an input that fails validation shall not terminate the application or change existing records. The application shall remain ready to accept the next command.

NFR-09. Data persistence: Following successful data changes and a normal application exit, WatchDog shall restore the updated dog records, customer records and their associations when reopened.

NFR-10. Privacy: WatchDog shall store dog and customer records locally and shall not transmit their contents to external services.

Glossary

  • WatchDog: The application used to manage a dog daycare centre's customer and dog information.
  • User: The person operating WatchDog, primarily the dog daycare manager.
  • Customer: A person whose contact details are recorded because they use the daycare's services for their dog or dogs.
  • Dog record: The information stored about an individual dog, including its ID, name, breed and size.
  • Customer record: The information stored about a customer, including their ID, name, primary contact number, email and backup contact number.
  • Dog ID: A unique integer assigned by WatchDog to identify a dog record. Dogs with the same name are distinguished by their IDs.
  • Customer ID: A unique integer assigned by WatchDog to identify a customer record. Customers with the same name are distinguished by their IDs.
  • List index: A record's position in the currently displayed list. Unlike a record ID, this position may change when the list is filtered or reordered.
  • Dog-customer association: The recorded relationship indicating which customer owns a dog.
  • Primary contact number: The customer's main telephone number, used as the first contact option.
  • Backup contact number: An alternative telephone number used when the customer cannot be reached through their primary number.
  • Breed: The recorded breed or breed mix of a dog, such as Chihuahua, Shiba Inu or a mixed breed.
  • Dog size: The dog's recorded size category: small, medium, large or giant, according to the size chart adopted by the team.
  • Scheduled attendance: A planned visit by a dog to the daycare. A scheduled dog is not necessarily currently present.
  • Activity: A care task planned for a dog, such as feeding, grooming or exercise.

Use a disposable data folder for this test. Substitute the computer's current local date for TODAY, the preceding date for YESTERDAY, and the following date for TOMORROW.

  2. Add two dogs and one customer; leave both dogs unowned and the customer without dogs. Add five schedules: one-time yesterday, today and tomorrow, daily starting yesterday, and weekly starting today. Use non-overlapping times for the today and daily schedules.
