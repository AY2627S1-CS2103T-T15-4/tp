Project: WatchDog

WatchDog is a desktop application that helps dog daycare managers manage customers, dogs, ownership relationships, and activity schedules. It combines keyboard commands with a JavaFX graphical interface and stores records locally.

Alongside implementation, I took an active role in coordinating the team's work, clarifying product decisions, and supporting teammates with technical questions. My main implementation contributions were activity creation and persistence, customer-dog linking and unlinking, and daycare statistics, with additional work on code quality, testing, and UI integration.

Given below are my contributions to the project.

  • Code contributed: RepoSense code dashboard.

  • New feature: Activity schedules and the addactivity command

    • Related PRs: #93 (model, creation, recurrence, and persistence); #127 (restoration consistency and boundary tests); #145 (parser refactoring and command contracts); #177 (positive activity IDs).
    • What it does: Creates activities with a name, date, start and end times, and recurrence rule. Activities have persistent IDs and can be saved and restored across application sessions.
    • Why it matters: Managers can define reusable schedules instead of repeatedly entering the same activity. This provides the scheduling foundation used by the team's enrolment and activity-management features.
    • Design and coordination challenge: Our team discussions established the intended final activity view, while the underlying model design and implementation were my responsibility. Activities differed substantially from customer and dog records: they needed time ranges, recurrence rules, and support for dated bookings. I had to translate the agreed user-facing view into a model structure and API before teammates could build their commands on it. This required planning how the parts would fit together, since later interface changes could affect several teammates and require coordinated rework.
    • Implementation highlights: Implemented immutable activity schedules and supporting value types, recurrence calculations, command parsing and validation, model integration, and JSON storage. Kept schedule rules in the activity model, input syntax in the parser, and persistent identity allocation in the model operation. Invalid creation requests do not consume an activity ID.
    • Technical challenges: Calculated recurring occurrences on demand, including start-date and date-range boundaries, without storing an unbounded list of future dates. Preserved the next unused ID across reloads and rejected fractional or textual JSON IDs before automatic conversion could hide invalid input.
    • Recurrence design decision: Chose one stored schedule per recurring series, with separate bookings identified by activity ID, dog ID, and occurrence date. This avoids generating unlimited future records or maintaining an arbitrary booking horizon, while allowing dogs to be booked independently for each occurrence. I also helped define the edit and deletion rules for teammates implementing those commands: a series with enrolments cannot be edited, and deleting a series removes its associated bookings. These rules keep references consistent without requiring booking migration or per-occurrence schedule exceptions, at the cost of less flexible editing and removal of past bookings when a series is deleted.

  • Integration improvement: Consistent restoration of activities and bookings

    • Related PRs: #127 (notification ordering); #205 (snapshot validation and boundary tests).
    • What it does: Validates incoming snapshots before replacing live records and restores activity schedules together with their associated enrolments.
    • Why it matters: Loading or restoring related data must preserve valid references and give observers a consistent view of the restored schedules and bookings.
    • Technical challenges: JavaFX listeners can run midway through restoration and observe new schedules with old bookings or counters. I ordered updates and batched activity notifications so listeners see consistent activity and enrolment data. Validating snapshots in an isolated candidate model also prevents invalid bookings from partially replacing live data. Tests check both the restored values and what listeners observe during updates.

  • New feature: Customer-dog ownership with link and unlink

    • Related PRs: #83 (commands, model/storage integration, and ownership display); #155, #205 (ownership invariants); #177 (space-separated dog IDs).
    • What it does: Links multiple dogs to a customer in one command. Removes ownership links by dog IDs or customer ID while retaining the underlying customer and dog records.
    • Why it matters: Managers can maintain ownership information separately from record creation and deletion, including handling customers with several dogs and dogs without an assigned owner.
    • Implementation highlights: Added model-level ownership checks and integrated relationship information into customer and dog cards. My contribution to these shared components focused on ownership relationships and their display.
    • Technical challenges: A missing or already-owned dog must reject the entire batch, even if earlier dogs are valid. I prepared replacements separately and published them only after every check passed, using persistent IDs independently of display filters. Ownership checks also protect model replacement and restoration paths that bypass command parsing.

  • New feature: Daycare statistics with summary

    • Related PRs: #144 (command and reusable enrolment query); #145, #205 (contracts and boundary tests); #220 (persistence/history integration tests and manual-testing guidance).
    • What it does: Shows customer and dog totals, total activity schedules, schedules with an occurrence today or later, today's activities, and distinct dogs enrolled today.
    • Why it matters: Gives managers an overview of their records and today's bookings without manually counting across lists or changing the current view.
    • Implementation highlights: Counts from the full model regardless of active filters and preserves records, filters, selection, and undo/redo history. Added a reusable model query for the distinct dogs enrolled on a supplied date.
    • Technical challenges: Distinguished schedule counts from dated bookings and deduplicated dogs by persistent ID, including dogs with identical names. Captured the date once to keep all statistics consistent across midnight, with clock-controlled tests for date changes, recurrence boundaries, and filtered records.

  • Earlier feature work: Search

    • Related PRs: #60 (substring-matching helper); #69 (early dog-card display); #74 (search commands, result display, tests, and guides); #88 (shared parser validation); #155 (global-search retirement).
    • Implemented early dog, customer, and combined search functionality using case-insensitive substring matching, with result cards and tests.
    • Consolidated shared search-field validation across parsers to reduce duplication (#88).
    • Design choice: Considered exact matching and substring matching. I chose substring matching so managers could find records using part of a name without remembering or typing the full name. This required defining and testing partial-match and case-handling behaviour beyond a simple equality check.
    • Wrote the corresponding syntax, examples, and behaviour documentation. These earlier command forms were subsequently retired as the team revised search for the expanded product. The replacement search commands were implemented by a teammate.

  • Testing and improvements to existing features

    • Implemented the early dog-card display alongside customer cards, connected it through Logic, and added UI tests. This supported displaying both record types before the team's later layout changes (#69).
    • Added tests for activity validation, recurrence, identity allocation, persistence, and restoration; ownership validation and batch operations; and summary counting and integration.
    • Covered boundary cases such as valid times at the edges of a day, invalid stored identities, and multiple bookings of the same dog across activities when calculating statistics.
    • Fixed identity comparison so separate dogs awaiting persistent IDs are not treated as the same record (#205).
    • Improved input consistency with space-separated dog IDs and positive activity IDs (#177).
    • Corrected command equals/hashCode consistency and refactored activity parsing into focused helpers (#145).
    • Added summary checks involving persisted data and undo/redo history, and made its manual-testing instructions reproducible (#220).
    • Improved diagnostics around activities, ownership, and saving (#93, #205). Fixed Help window reopening behaviour (#177) and strengthened startup and lifecycle tests (#196), with further Help window test stabilisation in #220.

  • Product design decisions

    • Daily planning: Proposed an overview showing all customers and dogs alongside today's activities, so managers could review the day's workload and plan staffing without first searching for the day's schedule. The final UI provides a dedicated Today panel alongside the broader activity panel; reset shows all customers and dogs and activities occurring today or later.
    • Related search results: Defined how filtering/search and the earlier selection commands should show related records across categories: matching customers with their dogs and activities, or matching dogs with their owners and activities. This helps managers follow relationships without repeatedly searching or remembering IDs. Teammates implemented the replacement search commands.
    • Activity-card content: Prioritised showing the dogs booked into an activity so managers could see who was participating. The final cards distinguish today's bookings from upcoming bookings, making the date context explicit for recurring schedules.
